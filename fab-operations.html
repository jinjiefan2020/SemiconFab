Physical Journey & Tiers

Fab Operations Architecture Map

A high-level synthesis of physical wafer progression and multi-tier supporting operational infrastructure.

Map Node Text Inventory (Accessible Alternative)
Physical Wafer Journey (8 Steps)
  1. Wafer In: Bare silicon wafer receiving and FOUP barcode registration.
  2. Lithography: Photolithography track coating, exposure, and development.
  3. Etch: Reactive ion etching (RIE) to transfer circuit geometries.
  4. Deposition: Thin-film formation (CVD, ALD, PVD, epitaxy).
  5. CMP: Chemical mechanical planarization ensuring global surface flatness.
  6. Metrology & Inspection: Optical/CD-SEM measurement and defect detection (cyclic loop).
  7. OSAT Packaging: Back-end grinding, laser dicing, wire bonding, molding, and testing.
  8. Shipment: Final packaged integrated circuits delivered to logistics docks.
4 Supporting Architectural Tiers
  • Tier 1: Operations Core: Manufacturing execution (MES), lot dispatching, automated material handling (AMHS), run-to-run control (APC), and scheduling benchmarks.
  • Tier 2: Data & Connectivity: Equipment automation programs (EAP), SECS/GEM and GEM300 industrial protocols, EDA Interface A high-speed telemetry, and 5-tier architecture.
  • Tier 3: Facility & Subfab: Ultra-pure water (UPW) loops, specialty and bulk gas manifolds, cleanroom HVAC air circulation with FFUs, dry vacuum pumps, and abatement scrubbers.
  • Tier 4: Business, Cost & Governance: Tool capex estimators, cleanroom construction cost breakdowns, capacity bottlenecks, consumable supply chain, and data provenance verification.

System Engineering

How Operations Topics Connect

Understanding the shop floor requires looking across three concurrent lifelines: materials, data, and decisions.

1. Material Flow (Physical Wafer & FOUP Handling)

Bare silicon wafers enter docking bays, are sealed inside Front Opening Unified Pods (FOUPs), and transfer via Automated Material Handling Systems (AMHS / OHT overhead hoists) across cleanroom bays. Batches undergo hundreds of repetitive deposition, lithography, etch, and CMP passes before passing to OSAT dicing, packaging, and final testing. (Teaching overview; material flows in the 3D twin are illustrative.)

2. Data Flow (Tool Telemetry, Connectivity & MES)

Each processing tool produces millions of real-time sensor parameters, state triggers, and metrology points. Standardized SEMI SECS/GEM, GEM300, and EDA/Interface A pipelines stream these signals through Equipment Automation Programs (EAP) into MES and yield management systems. For a detailed breakdown, see the dedicated Fab Data Layer.

3. Decision Flow (Dispatching, Control & Economics)

Real-time dispatching and scheduling engines balance WIP queues against machine availability, preventive maintenance windows, and customer priority constraints. Run-to-run (APC) algorithms calculate recipe feedback corrections, while planners evaluate total tool capex and cycle time. For scheduling simulation algorithms, explore the MIMAC-2026 Scheduling Benchmark.

Disclaimer: In this teaching overview, material, data, and decision flows are explained conceptually. There is no live equipment connection or production synchronization.

Questions & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a digital twin of an actual operating fab?
No. This is an educational synthesis model created by the SemiconFab initiative. It illustrates the holistic structure of wafer manufacturing and facility engineering, but has no live connection to any commercial fab and does not represent proprietary fab operating data.
Why are some topics marked as Planned?
To prevent dead links and fabricated content. SemiconFab strictly distinguishes between what has already been built and what is currently planned. Planned topics only define their intended engineering scope without pretending to have live content or pointing to non-existent pages.
Will commercial sponsorships affect topic ranking or objectivity?
No. This hub page permanently contains no sponsor banners, no vendor logos, and no commercial endorsements. If future dedicated technical topics receive non-commercial sponsorship, it will follow explicit disclosure guidelines without altering topic hierarchy, ranking, or objectivity.
How can university instructors use this page in class?
Instructors can use this page as a structured syllabus companion for semiconductor manufacturing, industrial engineering, or microelectronics courses. Walk students through the Wafer Journey, dive into Facility & Utility systems, explore the Data Layer, or assign scheduling experiments on the benchmark fab.
Where can data sources and technical credibility be verified?
SemiconFab operates under explicit engineering standards and open data policies. Please review our Data Standard for open JSON schemas and our Verification Policy for multi-source provenance grading criteria.
